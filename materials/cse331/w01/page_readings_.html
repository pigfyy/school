CSE 331: Readings

We will do four readings this quarter. For each one, you should choose one of the three article options we present, read it, and write a short reflection. Reflections are due on a Friday, and we will then discuss the readings the following Monday.

Schedule

  • Reading 1: due Friday, October 2; discussed Monday, October 5.
  • Reading 2: due Friday, October 16; discussed Monday, October 19.
  • Reading 3: due Friday, October 30; discussed Monday, November 2.
  • Reading 4: due Friday, November 20; discussed Monday, November 23.

Reflection Content

After reading your article, write a reflection with these three parts:

  1. What does the reading argue? Explain the author's main claim and how they support it, in your own words. Reconstruct the argument, not just the conclusion. Be specific enough that a classmate who read a different article could follow. (About 5 sentences.)

  2. What is your reaction? Which parts did you agree with? Disagree with? Are you unconvinced or unsure about any parts? Connect the paper to your own experience as a programmer. Would this paper have helped you with a difficult bug? We welcome your hot takes! (About 5 sentences.)

  3. What do you want to discuss in class? This could be something the reading left unresolved, something you would challenge the author on, or something you want to hear your classmates' views on. (1 or more discussion items.)

AI use

Reflections are your own thinking in your own words.

Read the article yourself, and write the reflection yourself.

You may use AI for light editing: grammar, spelling, trimming. If you do, say so on the submission form.

The point of a reflection is practice at reading an argument and deciding what you think of it. You only get that practice by doing it. It is also what you bring to Monday's discussion.

Submitting AI-written text as your reflection is academic misconduct. See Academic Integrity.

The syllabus has the course's full AI policy.

In-class discussion

We discuss each reading in lecture the Monday after it is due. You will talk about your article in a small group. Others in your group may have read a different article, so be ready to explain yours. Be ready to discuss and debate your thoughts on the topic too.

We will take 15 to 20 minutes of lecture time to discuss the readings in small groups. Each group will hand in a piece of paper with their group members' names and a brief summary of their discussion or a question they want to raise to the whole class. The sheet counts as an in-class activity for everyone named on it (see Grading).

Grading

We will grade your reflections based on "thoughtful completion". All three parts should be done with care, in your own thinking. A reflection that only goes through the motions is not really complete. Each of the three parts is marked Complete, Partial, or Missing.

The late policy applies to reflections as it does to homework.
