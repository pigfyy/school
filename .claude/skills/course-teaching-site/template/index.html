How many 5-letter strings use 5 different letters?

Type a word into the slots. Each slot has fewer choices because earlier letters are used up.

That one strip of slots is the whole of Lecture 1. Count by breaking an outcome into a sequence of choices, write down how many options each choice has given the earlier ones, and multiply. Most of the work is picking the right sequence.

Teaching notes: opening the course
  • Lecture 1 frames the course as counting → probability → properties of probability → continuous probability → applications. Counting comes first because discrete probability is counting: \(P(\text{black hair}) = \frac{\#\text{ students with black hair}}{\#\text{ students}}\).
  • Where randomness shows up in CS: modeling inputs (ML, fuzzing, algorithm analysis), validating designs (user studies), hiding information (crypto, privacy, fault tolerance), and simpler, faster designs (hashing, randomized algorithms).
  • The motivating questions from the slides: "How many ways to assign 7 TAs to 5 sections, two TAs per section, no TA in more than two sections?" and "How many positive integer solutions \((x,y,z)\) does \(x^3+y^3=z^3\) have?" (Zero. That's Fermat's Last Theorem for \(n=3\), so counting can be very hard.) Every counting question comes down to finding \(|S|\) for some set \(S\).

Lecture 1 · Today · Wed Sept 30 · Reading 1.1

The product rule

Product rule. In a sequential process with \(m\) steps, if there are \(n_1\) choices for step 1, \(n_2\) choices for step 2 given the first choice, …, and \(n_m\) choices for step \(m\) given the previous choices, the total number of outcomes is

\[n_1 \cdot n_2 \cdots n_m.\]

The options at each step can be different sets. What has to stay fixed is how many there are, no matter what was chosen earlier.

Binary strings of length \(n\) over \(\{0,1\}\): \(2\cdot 2\cdots 2 = 2^n\).

Cartesian products

Choosing a pair \((a,b)\in S\times T\) is a two-step process: pick \(a\) (\(|S|\) ways), then \(b\) (\(|T|\) ways). So

\[|S\times T| = |S|\cdot|T| \qquad |A_1\times\cdots\times A_n| = |A_1|\cdots|A_n|.\]

Example: \(\{1,2,3\}\times\{\star,\spadesuit\} = \{(1,\star),(2,\star),(3,\star),(1,\spadesuit),(2,\spadesuit),(3,\spadesuit)\}\), which has \(3\cdot 2 = 6\) elements.

Power sets: \(|2^S| = 2^{|S|}\)

To build a subset of \(S = \{s_1,\dots,s_n\}\), go through the elements one at a time and decide in or out. That's \(n\) steps with 2 choices each. Every subset matches exactly one in/out string, so subsets correspond to binary strings of length \(n\).

Click an element to put it in or out of the subset.

Quick check: how many subsets does \(\{a,b,c,d\}\) have?

Teaching notes
  • Stress "given the previous choices". The count at each step must not depend on which earlier option was picked, only that one was. Section task 7 (birthday cake) is built to break that if you go in the obvious order.
  • Ask: "How many subsets does \(\varnothing\) have?" \(2^0 = 1\): just \(\varnothing\) itself. The slide writes \(2^{\varnothing} = \{\varnothing\}\).
  • Ask the room to list \(2^{\{a,b\}}\) before showing it: \(\{\varnothing, \{a\}, \{b\}, \{a,b\}\}\). Students often forget \(\varnothing\) or the whole set.

Permutations

"How many sequences in \(\{1,2,3\}^3\) have no repeated element?" The first entry has 3 options. The second has 2, though which 2 depends on the first. The third has 1. The sets change but the counts don't, so the product rule gives \(3\cdot2\cdot1 = 6\).

Factorial

Permutations. The number of ways to order the \(n\) elements of a set is

\[n! = n\cdot(n-1)\cdots 2\cdot 1, \qquad 0! = 1.\]

Counting with complements

How many sequences in \(\{1,2,3\}^3\) do have a repeat? Every sequence either has a repeat or doesn't, never both, so by the sum rule

\[27 = 3^3 = \#\text{repeats} + \#\text{no repeats} = \#\text{repeats} + 3! \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \#\text{repeats} = 27 - 6 = 21.\]

Turn on "show all 27" above to see the 21 red leaves.

\(k\)-permutations

Sequences of 5 distinct letters (AZURE, BINGO, TANGO, but not STEVE or SARAH): \(26\cdot25\cdot24\cdot23\cdot22 = 7{,}893{,}600\). In general:

\(k\)-permutations. The number of \(k\)-element sequences of distinct symbols from an \(n\)-element set is

\[P(n,k) = n\cdot(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1) = \frac{n!}{(n-k)!}.\]

Quick check: 10 runners finish a race. How many ways can gold, silver and bronze be awarded?

Teaching notes
  • Why \(0! = 1\): there is exactly one way to order nothing (the empty sequence). It also makes \(\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\) work when \(k=n\).
  • \(P(n,n) = n!\), so the permutation formula is the special case \(k=n\).
  • Students mix up \(n^k\) (repeats allowed) and \(P(n,k)\) (no repeats). The home page widget has an "allow repeated letters" switch that shows both.

Check your understanding

Questions on everything from Lecture 1. Answer without looking back, then use the review links for anything you missed. Your score counts first tries only.

  1. Which of these is equal to the set \(\{1,2,3\}\)?

  2. What is \(\left|\,2^{\{a,b,c\}} \times \{0,1\}\,\right|\)?

    \(|2^{\{a,b,c\}}| = 2^3 = 8\) subsets, and \(|S\times T| = |S|\cdot|T|\), so \(8\cdot 2 = 16\).

  3. Which of these can you not count with the product rule, in the order given?

  4. How many 4-digit PINs (digits 0–9, leading zeros allowed) have at least one repeated digit?

    Count the complement. All PINs: \(10^4 = 10{,}000\). No repeats: \(10\cdot9\cdot8\cdot7 = 5{,}040\). So \(10{,}000 - 5{,}040 = 4{,}960\).

Section problems

Answer each problem directly on the page, and use the work box for your reasoning. Everything saves in this browser as you type. Hints and solutions stay hidden until you ask for them.

Review 1: sum rule

If you can choose from either one of \(n\) options or one of \(m\) options with no overlap with the previous \(n\), the number of possible outcomes is ___.

  1. Two groups that don't overlap. Are you picking one from each group, or one thing overall?
  1. Every outcome is in exactly one of the two groups, so count each group and add.
  2. \(n + m\)

Review 5: set difference

Is it always true that \(|A\setminus B| = |A| - |B|\)? Answer yes or no, and explain in your work.

  1. Try tiny sets. What happens if \(B\) has elements that aren't in \(A\)?
  1. Counterexample: \(A=\{1\}\), \(B=\{2,3\}\). Then \(|A\setminus B| = 1\), but \(|A|-|B| = -1\), and a size can't be negative.
  2. In general, only the part of \(B\) inside \(A\) gets removed: \(|A\setminus B| = |A| - |A\cap B|\). That's the sum rule, since \(A\) splits into the disjoint pieces \(A\setminus B\) and \(A\cap B\).
  3. No. It holds exactly when \(B\subseteq A\), because then \(A\cap B = B\).

Task 1a: cardinalities

Give the size of each set.

  1. Count only the top-level elements: the things separated by commas inside the outermost braces.
  2. \(\varnothing\) is an object too. A box holding an empty box isn't empty.
  1. \(|A| = 0\). The empty set has no elements.
  2. \(|B| = 1\). Its one element is \(\varnothing\).
  3. \(|C| = 1\). Its one element is \(\{\varnothing\}\). Nesting doesn't add elements at the top level.
  4. \(|D| = 2\). Its elements are \(\varnothing\) and \(\{\varnothing\}\), which are different objects.

Task 1b: operations

Let \(S=\{a,b,c\}\) and \(T=\{c,d\}\). Compute each set. (Check yourself against the solution; sets can be written in many orders.)

  1. \(S\setminus T\) keeps what's in \(S\) but not in \(T\). The power set of a 2-element set has \(2^2\) elements.
  2. \(S\times T\) pairs every element of \(S\) with every element of \(T\). Don't skip a pair whose two coordinates are the same.
  1. \(S\cup T = \{a,b,c,d\}\), and \(S\cap T = \{c\}\).
  2. \(S\setminus T = \{a,b\}\).
  3. \(2^{S\setminus T} = \{\varnothing, \{a\}, \{b\}, \{a,b\}\}\), which has 4 elements.
  4. \(S\times T = \{(a,c),(a,d),(b,c),(b,d),(c,c),(c,d)\}\), which has \(3\cdot 2 = 6\) elements.

Task 2b: divisors of 1440

How many positive divisors does \(1440 = 2^5\,3^2\,5\) have?

  1. Every divisor looks like \(2^a3^b5^c\). What values can each exponent take?
  2. Don't forget that an exponent can be 0.
  1. Every divisor has the form \(2^a3^b5^c\) with \(0\le a\le5\), \(0\le b\le2\), \(0\le c\le1\), and different exponent triples give different divisors.
  2. Choose \(a\) (6 options), then \(b\) (3), then \(c\) (2).
  3. \(6\cdot3\cdot2 = 36\)

Task 2d: Anup at an end or in the middle

How many ways are there to arrange the staff in a line so that Anup is at one of the ends or exactly in the middle?

  1. Place Anup first. Which spots is he allowed in? (The middle of 13 spots is spot 7.)
  1. Anup's allowed positions are 1, 7 and 13: 3 options.
  2. Arrange the other 12 people in the remaining 12 spots: \(12!\).
  3. \(3\cdot 12! = 1{,}437{,}004{,}800\)
Teaching notes
  • This is the first "place the constrained thing first" problem. Ask what happens if you fill spots left to right instead: spot 1's options depend on whether Anup still has to go somewhere.
  • In 2b, point out that the "steps" don't have to be physical. Choosing an exponent is a step.

Week 1 cheat sheet

Print with Ctrl+P; only this page prints.

Sum rule

Disjoint options: \(|A\cup B| = |A|+|B|\) when \(A\cap B=\varnothing\).

Complement: \(\#\text{good} = \#\text{all} - \#\text{bad}\).

Product rule

\(n_1\cdot n_2\cdots n_m\), where each \(n_i\) is the number of choices given the earlier ones and doesn't depend on which ones were made.

\(|A_1\times\cdots\times A_n| = |A_1|\cdots|A_n|\)

Standard counts

Strings of length \(k\) over \(n\) symbols\(n^k\)
Subsets of an \(n\)-set\(2^n\)
Orderings of \(n\) items\(n!\)
\(k\) distinct items in order from \(n\)\(\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\)
Functions \(A\to B\)\(|B|^{|A|}\)

Set facts

\(|A\setminus B| = |A| - |A\cap B|\)

\((A\cup B)^c = A^c\cap B^c\), \((A\cap B)^c = A^c\cup B^c\)

\(|\varnothing| = 0\), \(|\{\varnothing\}| = 1\)

Strategies

  • Glue "must be together" items into blocks.
  • Place the most constrained item first.
  • Start from a fixed value and work outward.
  • Split into disjoint cases and add.
  • Check with a second method or a small example.
